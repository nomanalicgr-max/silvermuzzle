How to Make Your Home Safer for a Senior Dog

You do not need to rebuild your house. Most of the risk for a senior dog is in three places: slippery floors, stairs, and dark hallways. Fix those, and the rest is detail.

1. Give paws something to grip

Put a rug runner on the route your dog uses most — bed to water bowl to door. Make sure rugs have a non-slip backing or a grip pad underneath, or they slide and become a hazard themselves. See Home Safety for options.

2. Block the stairs if needed

If your dog is unsteady, a gate at the top of the stairs is worth more than any gadget. Choose a pressure-mounted gate that fits your doorway, and check it stays firm.

3. Light the night route

Older eyes see less well in the dark. A motion-sensor night light in the hallway, and one near the garden door, helps your dog find their way at 3 a.m. without waking the house.

4. Clear the paths

Move trailing cables, shoes and clutter off the main walking routes. Keep water, food and bed in the same places — routine helps a dog who cannot see or hear as well as they used to.

5. Make the bed and sofa reachable

A ramp or low steps beside the sofa or bed means your dog does not have to jump. Read Ramp vs Stairs to choose.

6. Check the garden

Fill holes, block gaps in fences, and make sure the step out of the back door is not a trip hazard. A ramp over one high step can help.

Ramps, Stairs and Mobility Aids

Jumping gets harder with age. A ramp or a set of steps lets an older dog reach the car, sofa or bed without that jolt on landing.

What to look for

  • Weight limit: choose a ramp rated well above your dog's weight.
  • Grip: a textured or carpeted surface helps paws hold on, especially in rain.
  • Length and slope: longer ramps are gentler. Measure the height you need to reach.
  • Weight of the ramp itself: if you will carry it daily, a lighter folding ramp is kinder on your back too.

Common questions

Do I need the most expensive option?

Usually not. Match the size and weight limit to your dog first, then choose the best you can afford in that size. A well-fitting mid-price option beats a premium one in the wrong size.

What if my dog will not use it?

Introduce it slowly, with treats, and let your dog investigate it at their own pace. Never force them. If they still refuse after a few days, it may be the wrong shape or size — check the return policy before you buy.

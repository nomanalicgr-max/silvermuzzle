10 Signs Your Dog Is Entering Their Senior Years

Dogs do not become senior overnight. The changes are usually small, and they creep in over months. Noticing them early means you can make small adjustments — a better bed, shorter walks, a rug on a slippery floor — before they become problems.

These are common changes owners notice. They are not a diagnosis. If you are worried about any of them, mention it to your vet.

  1. Slower on walks. Your dog still enjoys going out, but lags behind, or wants to turn for home sooner.
  2. Stiffer in the morning. Slow to get up after a sleep, then loosens up after a few minutes.
  3. Grey around the muzzle. The classic sign — and usually the least worrying one.
  4. Sleeping more. Longer naps, deeper sleep, less interest in staying up late with you.
  5. Less interest in jumping. Hesitating before the car, the sofa or the bed.
  6. Cloudy eyes or less keen hearing. Bumping into things in dim light, or not hearing you call from another room.
  7. Changes in weight. Gaining or losing weight without a change in food. Your vet can advise on this — we do not give diet advice.
  8. More accidents indoors. Needing to go out more often, or not quite making it to the door.
  9. Less patience with noise or fuss. Older dogs often prefer calm and routine.
  10. Thinner coat or drier skin. Grooming needs change with age; gentle brushing helps.

What to do next

Start with the easy wins: a supportive bed, a non-slip route through the house, and shorter, more frequent walks. Keep a note of changes and take it to your vet — a short list is more useful than a vague worry.

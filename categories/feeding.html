Feeding: Raised Bowls, Slow Feeders and Food Storage

Mealtimes should be calm. A raised bowl can mean less bending for a stiff neck, a slow feeder can slow a gulper down, and a good container keeps food fresh.

We do not give nutrition or diet advice here. What and how much your dog eats is a question for your vet.

What to look for

  • Bowl height: the rim should sit near your dog's chest, not at floor level and not up at chin height. Adjustable stands help.
  • Stability: a non-slip base stops the bowl sliding across the floor.
  • Slow-feeder design: shallow ridges slow eating without frustrating your dog. Avoid very deep puzzles for older dogs.
  • Easy to clean: stainless steel or ceramic bowls wash well and do not hold smells.

Common questions

Do I need the most expensive option?

Usually not. Match the size and weight limit to your dog first, then choose the best you can afford in that size. A well-fitting mid-price option beats a premium one in the wrong size.

What if my dog will not use it?

Introduce it slowly, with treats, and let your dog investigate it at their own pace. Never force them. If they still refuse after a few days, it may be the wrong shape or size — check the return policy before you buy.

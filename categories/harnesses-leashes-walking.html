Harnesses, Leashes and Walking Gear

A good harness spreads pressure across the chest and body instead of the neck, and gives you a handle for a steadying lift when your dog stumbles.

What to look for

  • Fit: measure chest and neck, and check the maker's size chart — sizes vary a lot between brands.
  • Padding: soft lining matters on thin skin and bony areas.
  • Handle: a top handle helps with steps, curbs and getting up.
  • Easy on and off: step-in or clip designs are kinder than over-the-head styles for stiff dogs.

Common questions

Do I need the most expensive option?

Usually not. Match the size and weight limit to your dog first, then choose the best you can afford in that size. A well-fitting mid-price option beats a premium one in the wrong size.

What if my dog will not use it?

Introduce it slowly, with treats, and let your dog investigate it at their own pace. Never force them. If they still refuse after a few days, it may be the wrong shape or size — check the return policy before you buy.

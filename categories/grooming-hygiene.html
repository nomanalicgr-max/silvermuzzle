Grooming and Hygiene

Older dogs tire faster, and their skin can be thinner. Short, gentle grooming sessions beat long ones — and the right tools make them easier for both of you.

What to look for

  • Soft bristles: gentle on skin, no scratching.
  • Lightweight tools: easier on your hands during a longer groom.
  • Waterless options: wipes and dry shampoo help between baths, when a full bath is too much.
  • Paw care: check paws regularly for cracks, and keep nails trimmed — long nails change how a dog walks.

Common questions

Do I need the most expensive option?

Usually not. Match the size and weight limit to your dog first, then choose the best you can afford in that size. A well-fitting mid-price option beats a premium one in the wrong size.

What if my dog will not use it?

Introduce it slowly, with treats, and let your dog investigate it at their own pace. Never force them. If they still refuse after a few days, it may be the wrong shape or size — check the return policy before you buy.

Senior Dog Slipping on Hardwood Floors? Practical Fixes

Hardwood looks lovely and is easy to clean. For an older dog with weaker legs, it can feel like an ice rink. The good news: you do not need to replace the floor.

Why it happens

Older dogs have less muscle strength, less grip from worn pads, and slower reactions. A smooth floor gives paws nothing to hold on to, so legs splay and confidence drops.

Fix 1: Rugs on the main routes

Start with a runner from bed to door, and a rug by the water bowl. Use a non-slip backing or grip pad — a sliding rug is worse than no rug. See Home Safety.

Fix 2: Paw grip socks or toe grips

Rubberised socks give grip indoors. Introduce them for short periods first — most dogs need a day or two to get used to them. Check the fit; socks that are too tight or too loose will come off.

Fix 3: Keep nails trimmed

Long nails change how a paw meets the floor and make slipping worse. If you are unsure how to trim them, ask a groomer or vet to show you.

Fix 4: Keep paws clean and dry

Wet paws slip more. A quick wipe after coming in from the garden helps, and a mat by the door catches most of it.

Fix 5: Slow down the house

Excited greetings on a slippery floor end badly. Greet your dog calmly, and keep the main route clear so they do not have to turn sharply.

If slipping is sudden, or your dog seems in pain, talk to your vet. This guide covers the home, not the cause.

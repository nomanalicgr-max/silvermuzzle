Orthopedic Beds and Comfort

A good bed is where comfort starts. Older dogs sleep more, and a bed that supports their weight evenly can make mornings less stiff and nights less restless.

What to look for

  • Foam thickness: 4 inches or more for medium and large dogs; thinner beds flatten quickly.
  • Washable cover: accidents happen. A removable, machine-washable cover matters more than a fancy pattern.
  • Non-slip base: so the bed stays put on hard floors.
  • Size: your dog should be able to stretch out fully. Measure them lying down, then add a few inches.
  • Bolsters or flat? Some dogs lean on a bolster; others prefer a flat mat they can step onto. Watch how yours sleeps.

Common questions

Do I need the most expensive option?

Usually not. Match the size and weight limit to your dog first, then choose the best you can afford in that size. A well-fitting mid-price option beats a premium one in the wrong size.

What if my dog will not use it?

Introduce it slowly, with treats, and let your dog investigate it at their own pace. Never force them. If they still refuse after a few days, it may be the wrong shape or size — check the return policy before you buy.

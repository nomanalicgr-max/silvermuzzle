How to Help an Older Dog Get Into the Car

The car used to mean a jump and a wag. Now it can mean a pause, a look at you, and a small struggle. Here is how to make it easy again.

Use a ramp

A folding ramp is the kindest option for most dogs. Choose one rated well above your dog's weight, with a non-slip surface. Introduce it at home first — flat on the ground, with treats — before you try it at the car. See our Best Dog Ramps for Older Dogs.

If you need to lift

Support the chest and hindquarters together, and lift in one smooth movement. A rear support sling can help for the back end. If lifting hurts your back, or your dog is heavy, a ramp is the better answer — do not risk an injury to either of you.

Make the car comfortable

A non-slip mat in the boot, a familiar blanket, and a little space to lie down help. Keep the car cool, and never leave a dog in a hot car.

Keep practising gently

Short, calm trips with a reward at the end keep the car a happy place. If your dog is very anxious about the car, talk to your vet — anxiety is a health question, not a training failure.
